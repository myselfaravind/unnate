the part most students are missing

The explanation guide

Not a folder of code you can’t explain.

Reviews and vivas are where projects are really judged. Examiners ask why you chose a database, what happens when a user logs in, how you would extend it. If you can’t answer, even a working project feels like someone else’s.

So every project comes with a guide written for you. It walks through the system the way you would explain it out loud, and we go through it with you before your review.

  • Written in plain language, section by section
  • Points to the exact files and functions it describes
  • Ends with practice questions, so you can test yourself

What happens when a patient books an appointment

When the patient submits the booking form, the frontend sends the details to the server. The server checks the time slot is still free, saves the booking, and queues a reminder.

  • Booking form
  • API route
  • Check the slot
  • Save to database
  • Send reminder

Where to find it

backend/routes/appointments.js, in createAppointment()

Q. Why check the slot again on the server?

Because two people could choose the same time at once. The browser can’t see other users’ bookings; only the server can.

How it works

From first message to review day, one clear step at a time.

You’ll always know what’s happening, what comes next and what we need from you.

  1. 1

    Share what you need

    Your department, your idea or abstract if you have one, your review dates, and any format your college has given you.

    You: send what you have
  2. 2

    Agree the scope

    We suggest features and technologies that fit your timeline and level, and agree the plan in writing before any work starts.

    You: approve the plan
  3. 3

    Build, with check-ins

    We build in stages and show you progress along the way, so you can ask questions while it’s taking shape, not after.

    You: review and ask
  4. 4

    Document and explain

    We write the report and the explanation guide around the finished system, so both describe what was actually built.

    You: read the guide
  5. 5

    Walkthrough and handover

    We go through running the project, the demo and the guide with you. You test it yourself until you’re comfortable with it.

    You: run it on your own

Learning, not shortcuts

Your project should still be yours.

We provide development support and help you learn the system. How you use outside support is governed by your college’s rules, so read them and keep your project guide informed.

What you can expect

  • Honest advice on a scope you can actually deliver
  • Readable code, so you can follow it and change it
  • Time to ask questions until things make sense
  • Documentation that describes the real system
  • Encouragement to customise, test and extend it yourself

What we won’t do

  • Fabricate research results, data or references
  • Promise marks, grades or placements
  • Help hide outside support where your college requires you to declare it
  • Take part in reviews or vivas on your behalf

    Questions students ask

    Good to know.

    Still unsure about something? Ask it in your enquiry and we’ll give you a straight answer.

    Which students do you work with?

    Final-year students in computer science, IT, AI and data science, and other engineering branches whose project is software-based, across degree programmes such as BE, BTech, BCA, MCA and MSc.

    Can I bring my own idea?

    Yes, and we encourage it. If you don’t have one yet, we’ll suggest a few that suit your interests, your department and your timeline.

    Will I really understand the project?

    That’s what the explanation guide and the walkthrough are for. The code is readable, the guide explains it in plain language, and you can ask questions at every stage.

    You’ll still need to put in the time to read it, run it and practise explaining it. That part is yours.

    Can the report follow my college’s format?

    Yes. Share your department’s guidelines or a sample report, and we’ll structure the documentation to match.

    How long does it take?

    It depends on the scope. Tell us your review and submission dates in your first message, and we’ll tell you honestly whether the timeline is realistic before you commit to anything.

    Do you help with group projects?

    Yes. Tell us how many members there are and how the work is split. The guide can explain each part, so every member can present their share.

    What does it cost?

    It depends on the scope of the project and what you need. Once we understand your requirements, we’ll give you a clear quote, before any work begins.

    Is this allowed by my college?

    Rules on outside help differ between institutions and departments. Check your college’s guidelines and talk to your project guide. We support you in building, understanding and presenting your project; we don’t help anyone misrepresent their work.
